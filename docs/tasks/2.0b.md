# 2.0b Grid strips

ADR-0011, rebuilt on 2.0a as landed (the prototype, `ca904cd`, predates
2.0a's review fixes). The grid's cells and the gutter's numbers are drawn
into strips of rows that scrolling moves. On the reference file the main
thread does 2.1–2.8 M instructions a frame at p50 against 6.3–9.0 M with
AppKit's drawing, and 6.1 M against 9.7 M at p99 (same binary,
alternated). Footprint peak is 24–74 MB higher, against 1.10's tiles'
+180 MB.

**Deviation, accepted by Rob 2026-10-03: the switch to AppKit's drawing is at 1.5 times the
visible width (never past 4,096 pt), not at 4,096 pt.** ADR-0011 said the
line "may need to be lower". Strips stop paying at 1.5–1.75 times the
visible width. At 3,720 pt in a 1,200 pt window they add 185 MB footprint
peak, and their p99 frames run 84 M instructions against AppKit's 11 M.
Going back to strips needs 1.4 times (3,840 pt). ADR-0011's Decision
now says so.

## What was built

- **`GridStrips`** (`app/Sources/Grid/GridStrips.swift`) draws a
  `StripContentView` (`GridView`, `GridGutterView`) into strips four rows
  tall and as wide as the view (rounded up to 256 pt, cut at the grid's
  edge by a clipping layer). Each strip is a `CALayer` drawn
  asynchronously, in a layer-hosting view over the clip view, under the
  scrollers.
  - A scroll moves the strips. It draws only those coming into view, plus
    up to 2 a frame a strip ahead; 2 spares keep their backing stores.
  - Positions are relative to the visible rectangle, so 880M pt grids stay
    exact.
- **The views draw nothing on screen with strips** (`wantsUpdateLayer`,
  empty layer; `layerContentsRedrawPolicy` `.never`). `needsDisplay = true`
  redraws every strip. **`setNeedsDisplay(_:)` is ignored**: it is how
  AppKit asks for the strip a scroll exposes. App code uses
  `invalidate(_:)`, which redraws a rectangle in every strip placed, ahead
  of the scroll too.
- **The same drawing**: `drawContent(in:context:)` is what `draw(_:)` was;
  strips call it translated to their rows, with the view's appearance.
- **Ink that spills past its row** (stacked marks). Lines know their ink
  bounds (`LaidOutLine.ink`, made off the main thread for lines laid out
  ahead). The grid learns which rows spill and how far (`GridView.spills`).
  A strip also draws rows outside it whose ink reaches it, cut to the
  strip, so strip edges don't cut ink. Ink learnt only when a row is
  drawn (first drawn after the strip it reaches, or an edit that makes it
  spill) shows in the next frame; an edit that stops a row spilling clears
  the rows it reached in the same pass (`cellsChanged`). Rows that don't spill cost
  one dictionary check per strip edge.
- **The line** (`GridContainerView.stripLine`) is checked whenever the
  grid's or the visible width changes: column resize, columns read, window
  resize. The gutter always has strips.
- **Snapshots go through the strips.** `cacheDisplay` on the grid view or
  gutter draws the strips' backing stores where the strips' view puts them
  (outside the clip view it draws directly). `cacheDisplay` on a container
  (scroll view, window frame for `just snapshot`) renders the strips'
  layers. A stale, blank or misplaced strip shows in tests and screenshots.
- **`GridOverlayView`** (`grid.overlay`): the in-cell editor's place (see
  "For 2.5").
- **Invalidation from the document**: `DocumentModel.cellsChanged(rows:)`
  now tells the window (`DocumentChange.cells`), which redraws those rows
  in every strip. Reload, Treat As, the Header row and a re-read drive
  already call `invalidateContent`. Any change of `model.readingID` (handle
  or generation) also redraws everything, whatever change brought it, so a
  save's new snapshot (2.5) is covered. That rule is tested only through
  Reload, Treat As and the Header row, since no app path yet changes the
  generation otherwise.
- **Bench and `leal-perf`**: `ScrollBench` reports process and
  off-main-thread CPU a frame, strips drawn a frame, the drawing, grid and
  visible widths, and when the scroll ran. `leal-perf` samples
  WindowServer's CPU with `ps` every 250 ms and reports its share a frame.
  `--compare-drawing` alternates every scroll run with AppKit's drawing
  (`-LealStrips NO`, bench builds only) and prints a comparison table.
  `--only-scroll` skips launch and open runs.

## Where strips pay

AppKit draws the visible area; a strip draws the grid's whole width. When a
fling jumps a screen after a late frame, every strip in view is drawn at
once, and those frames set the p99. Full scroll benchmark, one run each,
alternated, M5 Pro, 1,200 × 784 pt window on Retina at 120 Hz, visible
width 1,134 pt, 100,000-row files of long text columns. The Mac was loaded
(load 4–18, main thread 1.6–4.3 GHz), so compare instructions:

| Grid width (× visible) | Instructions p50 / p99, strips vs AppKit | Late | Footprint peak |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1,134 (1.0) | 2.0 / 5.4 vs 6.3 / 9.4 M | 4 vs 2 | 258 vs 222 MB |
| 1,230 (1.08, reference) | 2.1 / 6.1 vs 6.4 / 9.7 M | 1 vs 2 | 271 vs 224 MB |
| 1,511 (1.33) | 2.0 / 7.2 vs 6.3 / 9.8 M | 1 vs 5 | 280 vs 222 MB |
| 1,691 (1.49, the line) | 2.2–2.3 / 6.9–7.4 vs 6.4 / 9.7 M | 0, 17 vs 4 | 294–299 vs 225 MB |
| 1,752 (1.54) | 2.3 / 9.2 vs 6.3 / 9.5 M | 4 vs 8 | 294 vs 239 MB |
| 1,996 (1.76) | 2.2–2.3 / 9.5–30.7 vs 5.8–6.4 / 9.8–10.1 M | 2, 7, 35 vs 1, 3, 4 | 317–319 vs 232–236 MB |
| 2,729 (2.41) | 2.5 / 62–64 vs 6.4 / 9.9 M | 14, 69 vs 3 | 357–359 vs 236 MB |
| 3,720 (3.28) | 2.8 / 84 vs 5.9 / 10.8 M | 53 vs 4 | 423 vs 238 MB |

- Up to 1.5 times strips win: p50 a third of AppKit's, p99 two-thirds to
  three-quarters, no more late frames. Even at 1.75; well behind from 2.4.
- **Memory at the switch (Retina)**: at the line, +68–74 MB footprint peak
  (293.5 and 299.1 against 225.3 MB). Settled after scrolling, strips use
  less (99–104 against 114 MB). A full-screen window scales both, about 2×.
- Synchronous strips (`drawsAsynchronously` off) on the 3,720 pt grid:
  6× the instructions at p50, 162 late frames.

**Render server and other threads**, `leal-perf --only-scroll
--compare-drawing --runs 1`, reference file:

| Scroll run | Main p50 / p99 | Instr. (mean) | Other threads | WindowServer | Late | Footprint peak |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| After indexing, strips | 0.71 / 1.60 ms | 2.5 M | 1.23 ms | 1.97 ms | 5 | 277 MB |
| After indexing, AppKit | 1.16 / 1.72 ms | 6.3 M | 1.21 ms | 1.85 ms | 5 | 229 MB |
| Loading + search, strips | 0.87 / 2.07 ms | 2.8 M | 2.12 ms | 2.20 ms | 1 | 287 MB |
| Loading + search, AppKit | 2.08 / 3.89 ms | 9.0 M | 2.55 ms | 2.40 ms | 1 | 303 MB |

The clock was 4.2 GHz in the first pair and 2.1–2.8 GHz in the second,
and the screen locked during the run: these compare the two drawings, not
the budget. WindowServer's figure is its whole CPU over the scroll, the
rest of the screen included; compositing the strips costs about the same.

**For the final run: `just perf --compare-drawing`** (about 10 minutes
longer than `just perf`). It adds the "Drawing compared" table against
2.0a's drawing and WindowServer's share.

## Tests

25 new app tests (`GridStripsTests`, `GridStripsDocumentTests`; 208 in
all) and 3 in `leal-bench`.

- **Pixels**: strips against AppKit drawing the same grid, through
  `cacheDisplay`, sRGB windows, light and dark at 2× and 1×, scrolled down,
  sideways and to skeleton rows. The grid has a selection, find marks and
  the current match, hatching, a zero-width column, other scripts, emoji,
  and marks spilling several rows, put in each row of a strip in turn. The
  reference redraws ten more rows each way (AppKit draws only rows in
  view, so it misses ink spilling in). A channel may differ by one level
  (dark hatching's translucent colour rounds once more).
- **Snapshots go through the strips**: unannounced value changes stay
  stale; a hidden or 1 pt-moved strip shows in the grid view's and the
  grid's snapshot; a hidden gutter strip in the grid's; a hidden strip in
  the window's.
- **Flings**: 53 steps (900 pt a frame down to 1, back up, a jump to 1M
  pt, fractional, sideways, past the right edge), with selection and find
  changing mid-fling. Every step covers the visible rows and matches
  AppKit. A scroll within the strips, or sideways, draws nothing; spares
  are reused.
- **Selection** redraws two cells' area, not whole strips.
- **Column and window resizes**: step by step and across the line both
  ways (1.5×, back at 1.4×; 4,096/3,840 pt in a 3,200 pt window); the
  first width decides by the line itself. Live-resize steps that keep the
  strips' width draw only strips coming into view.
- **Display scale**: 2× ↔ 1× (scale injected, as AppKit tells the views):
  every strip takes the new `contentsScale`, is redrawn and matches.
- **VoiceOver frames**: each strip's window frame equals where the grid
  view says its rows are, at fractional offsets and at 880M pt; a cell's
  window frame is where its text is in the snapshot.
- **Overlay**: above the strips, under the scrollers, stays over its cell
  while scrolling (880M pt too), takes clicks only on itself.
- **Every change redraws**, in view and in the strip ahead: an edit
  (`cellsChanged`), values re-read, rows read, selection, find, dark,
  light. In a document against the core: the Header row, Treat As, Reload
  and `DocumentModel.cellsChanged`.
- **Against AppKit's own `draw(_:)`** (no spilling rows; the other
  comparisons use `drawDirectly`, which strips use too). **Edits** that
  start or stop spilling (stopping clears in the same frame). **System
  colours** redraw every strip. **A window narrowing in one step** keeps
  the same strips. **A new reading** redraws all, even when only an
  edit's rows are heard. **Closing the document** releases the strips.
- Two 2.0a tests now look 8 rows past the view for laid-out-ahead lines:
  the row just below is drawn by the strip ahead.

## For the on-screen check

Release build, built-in display at 120 Hz:

1. Fast flings, trackpad and scroller, on the reference file. Look for a
   blank or stale band for a frame, or a band a row off (asynchronous
   rasterising timing is invisible to tests). Also watch for a band
   showing the wrong rows, not only a blank one.
2. Elastic bounce at top, bottom and right edge of a wide grid.
3. A grid near 1.5× the window's width: drag a column, and resize the
   window, across the line. No flicker. `just wide-file` scrolls as before.
4. Move the window to the 1× display and back, also mid-drag: no blur, no
   wrong-size strips.
5. Dark/light, Increase Contrast, selection and search while scrolled;
   Reload, Treat As, the Header row.
6. Stacked-mark text scrolled slowly both ways (upward spill may be a
   frame late).
7. Activity Monitor's Memory at about 1.4× the window's width.
8. Change the accent colour (System Settings) while scrolled: cells,
   selection and gutter take it at once, with no stale strips.

## For 2.5

- The editor goes in `grid.overlay`: `overlay.show(editor, at:
  geometry.cellRect(row:column:))`, and `removeFromSuperview()` to end
  (`show` again to move it). It is an ordinary view: first responder, and
  marked text and the candidate window from its frame. The invalid-bytes
  callout goes there too.
- After an edit, undo or redo: `model.cellsChanged(rows:)`. Never
  `gridView.setNeedsDisplay(_:)` (ignored with strips); use `invalidate(_:)`
  or `needsDisplay = true`.
- 2.5a's row and column inserts: `invalidateContent` or `reloadData`.

## Scheduling (DESIGN §3.10)

No new queues. Strips are recorded on the main thread and rasterised on
Core Animation's own threads. That is not core work and never waits on the
core.

## Tried and not kept

- The 4,096 pt line (above).
- One extra row each side of every strip for spills: +50% drawing, and the
  test marks spill four rows.
- Strips exactly as wide as the grid: a live resize redrew all of them on
  every step.
- Synchronous strips (above).

## Rust notes

Only `leal-bench` (the measuring tool) changed.

- `parse_ps_time` and `cpu_between` (`crates/leal-bench/src/perf.rs`)
  return `Option<f64>`. Inside them, `?` returns `None` early, as it
  returns an error early in a `Result` function.
- `cpu_between(&[(f64, f64)], …)` borrows a slice: it reads the samples
  without taking ownership of the caller's `Vec`.
- `if let (Some(a), Some(b), Some(n)) = span && n > 0.0 && let Some(c) = …`
  in `scroll_run` is a let-chain (Rust 2024): every part must match, with
  no nesting.
- `ScrollRun`'s new fields are `Option`s, so runs saved before this task
  still read: missing means `None`, printed "—", never 0.
